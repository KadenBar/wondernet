# Software and Content Licensing — Pre-publication Decision

**Current repository software license: MIT.** It is already present in [LICENSE](../LICENSE), not just a proposed future file. MIT permits commercial reuse and closed-source derivatives when its conditions are followed.

**Before publication**, the owner should explicitly decide whether to keep MIT or switch *before accepting outside contributions* to a copyleft option (such as AGPL-3.0). Changing licenses later can be complex once external contributors arrive.

## Keep these layers distinct

- **Project source code**: MIT unless and until changed through a deliberate, documented decision.
- **Third-party dependencies**: retain their licenses and notices.
- **Lessons, books, audio, artwork and game media**: licensed or permissioned individually; software MIT alone does **not** grant rights to distribute them.
- **Administrator-imported material**: locally held by the operator, not redistributed by the upstream repository.
- **Name/logo**: “WonderNet” is a working name. Clear branding before broad promotion.

## Decisions to record

- [ ] Commercial use permitted? (MIT currently says yes.)
- [ ] Must improvements to network-served modifications be shared?
- [ ] Contributor terms / licensing attestation for assets?
- [ ] Approved sources for curriculum and bundled content?
- [ ] Trademark/name clearance?

This is a planning note, not legal advice.
